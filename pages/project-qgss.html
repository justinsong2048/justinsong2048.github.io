Why it matters

Every gate adds noise

On today's quantum processors, each gate has a small chance of error, and two-qubit gates are the worst. Not every pair of qubits is wired together either, so a circuit that looks short on paper can grow much deeper once it's mapped onto the real chip. Most of the work in these labs was fitting circuits to the hardware and then measuring and undoing the noise.

About the code

QGSS asks participants not to share coursework, so this page describes methods and results only. I'm happy to walk through the work in an interview.

What I did

Circuits, noise, mitigation

1 · Circuits

GHZ states that fit the chip

A GHZ state entangles every qubit in a register. IBM's heavy-hex layout connects each qubit to only two or three neighbors, and every SWAP to bridge a gap costs three more two-qubit gates. So I built the entangling order from a breadth-first-search spanning tree of the chip's coupling map, growing outward from a root qubit along real connections.

That cut a 64-qubit GHZ circuit's transpiled depth from 256 layers to 44, and reached the optimal depth on a 7-qubit T-junction. I also built dynamic GHZ circuits that use mid-circuit measurement and feedforward.

2 · Noise

Modeling where errors come from

In simulation, I modeled three kinds of noise: depolarizing error, Pauli error, and thermal relaxation set by each qubit's T1 and T2 times. I fit depolarizing error rates to within 0.5% using SciPy. I then compared T1/T2, gate and readout errors across 59 IBM backend models from 2017 to 2025, to see how the hardware has improved.

3 · Mitigation

Error mitigation on ibm_fez

On the real 156-qubit processor, I ran noise learning and then error-mitigated jobs on a 6-qubit Ising mirror circuit. I configured each technique through Qiskit Runtime:

  • Dynamical decoupling to protect idle qubits
  • Pauli twirling to turn coherent errors into random ones
  • TREX for readout errors
  • Zero-noise extrapolation, which runs at amplified noise and extrapolates back to zero
  • Probabilistic error cancellation, which uses the learned noise model
Takeaway

Why this points me at hardware

The labs made it clear that most of what limits quantum computers today is physical: wiring, coherence time and calibration. That's the part I want to work on as a mechanical engineer.
