Work / Adaptive cruise control

Adaptive cruise control simulation

A MATLAB model of a car that follows another car on its own. A tuned PD controller sets the follower's acceleration to hold a safe gap through steady traffic, a hard stop and stop-and-go swings.

0Collisions across 3 traffic scenarios
~30 sTo close a 400 ft gap to the 180 ft target
≤ 11%Gap error (about 20 ft) in fluctuating traffic
The model

Two cars on one line

We modeled the road as one dimension with a lead car and a follower. Each time step, the controller reads the gap error and how fast it's changing, then sets the follower's acceleration. We clamped acceleration to limits a real driver would accept, and capped speed.

Position and speed update with Euler integration over 1,200 steps of 0.05 s, which is 60 seconds of driving.

e = gap − 180 ft
a = Kp·e + Kd·ė
−15 ≤ a ≤ +10 ft/s² Kp = 0.1, Kd = 0.7
vk+1 = vk + a·Δt
xk+1 = xk + vk·Δt Δt = 0.05 s, 1,200 steps
Results

Three scenarios

Select any plot to enlarge it.

Scenario 1

Constant speed

The lead car holds steady. The follower closes from 400 ft and settles at 180 ft in about 30 s.

Scenario 2

Hard brake, 55 → 0 mph

At 10 s the lead car brakes at 15 ft/s² from 55 mph to a stop. The follower brakes within its own −15 ft/s² limit and never touches it.

Scenario 3

±10 mph swings

The lead car swings between 50 and 70 mph on a 30 s sine wave. The gap stays within about 20 ft (11%) of the target.

Outcome

Safe in every scenario

The follower never collided in any of the three scenarios and stayed within its acceleration limits throughout.

What I'd change: I'd add sensor delay to the model, since a real radar or camera reports the gap a moment late, and compare the PD controller against a PID controller to see whether an integral term removes any steady-state gap error.
